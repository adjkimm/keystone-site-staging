For builders · Track B

Give your agents an identity merchants can check.

Your agents get blocked, rate limited, or treated like bots because merchants cannot tell them apart from scrapers. A signed credential changes that: your agent arrives with proof of who it is.

How issuing works

01

Register as an issuer

Publish your issuer identity and public key. No permission needed, no exclusive deals. The spec is open and the verifier treats all issuers equally.

02

Sign credentials for your agents

Each credential binds an agent to your issuer identity: who operates it, its public key, what it may do, when it expires. You can revoke any credential you issued, any time.

03

Your agents present them

The agent presents its credential on arrival. Merchants verify it in under a second. Your legitimate traffic stops looking like an attack.

Why an open spec

Identity infrastructure only works if it is neutral. If one company owned issuance, merchants would be right not to trust it. So the spec is open, issuance is permissionless, and our verifier checks every issuer by the same rules. Read the neutrality pledge.

Plainly stated: a credential proves origin, not behavior. Issuing a credential does not certify that your agent is safe, and presenting one does not oblige any merchant to admit your agent.

During the pilot

Builders on the waitlist get the one-page spec first, then access to issue demo credentials against the demo verifier. Real issuance tooling follows only if the pilot shows pull.
