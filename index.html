Signed identity for AI agents

AI agents are becoming real counterparties. Right now, nobody can verify who they are.

A merchant's logs show whatever an agent claims about itself in headers anyone can spoof. Project Keystone gives agents cryptographically signed identity credentials, and gives merchants a way to check them. Not what the agent says. What the math says.

The problem in one line

Agents browse, negotiate, and buy on behalf of users, but a merchant has no standard way to answer: who sent this agent, and is it who it claims to be?

Unverifiable counterparties

User-agent strings and headers are trivially faked. There is no standard way to prove which agent did what.

Abuse is cheap

Scraping, price manipulation, and fake orders all get easier when the attacker is software acting at scale. Merchants cannot tell a legitimate shopping agent from a hostile bot.

Liability is unpriced

When an agent makes a bad purchase or leaks data, nobody can prove which agent did it or who is responsible.

What we are building

01

Verify

A merchant presents an agent's credential and gets a signed yes or no: this agent is who it claims, issued by whom, still valid. The verifier is free, forever.

02

See

A dashboard showing which identified agents visited a store, what they looked at, and what they bought.

03

Monitor

Alerts when an unknown, revoked, or suspicious agent shows up.

Attestation, not a guarantee. We verify identity. We do not vouch for behavior. A credential that checks out means "this agent is who it claims to be," never "this agent is safe."
